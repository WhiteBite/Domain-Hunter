How to Find Domain Name Ideas That Are Actually Available

Every dictionary word in .com was registered decades ago. The names that remain registerable are invented — and invention is a technique, not inspiration. Here are the five methods that work, with examples.

1. Affix combinatorics: root × prefix/suffix

Take a root and cross it with productive affixes: karato + ai / hq / hub / lab / flow / forge → karato.ai, getkarato.com, karatohq.com. Two-way (prefix and suffix) crossing turns 3 roots and 20 affixes into 120 candidates in seconds.

2. Syllable mixing for pronounceable neologisms

Assemble 2–3 syllables from onset/rime banks and filter by phonotactics — no triple consonant clusters, no double vowels, sane endings. Score candidates with an n-gram pronounceability model. Results read like brands: short, sayable, spellable.

3. TLD hacks: make the extension part of the name

family → fami.ly, audio → au.dio-style splits. Matching word endings against hack-friendly zones (ly io me so ai sh ac cc tv co) turns ordinary words into short, memorable domains.

4. Mutations of real words

Vowel swap (midas → mydas), s↔z (midaz), truncation, suffixing (midaso). Misspelling on purpose is how many real brands got their names — and the variants are usually unregistered.

5. Thematic word sets with meaning

Curated categories — metals, forge words, speed, swarms, constellations, mythology — give semantically loaded roots (aurum, vega, forge) instead of random syllables. Each word carries a story you can build a brand on.
