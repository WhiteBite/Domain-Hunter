Domain Promo Traps Explained: How $1 First-Year Deals Cost More

You see .dev for $0.99 and think you found a deal. Then you renew three years later and the bill says $24.99. Welcome to the promo trap — one of the most common ways domain buyers overpay without realizing it.

Why $1 first-year deals exist

Registrars subsidize first-year prices to acquire customers. A $0.99 .com costs the registrar roughly $9.23 (Verisign's wholesale price). They lose money on year one hoping you will renew at a higher rate. This is a legitimate marketing strategy, not a scam — but it means the headline price is almost never the real price.

The renewal markup math

Here is a concrete example. You register mybrand.dev at three different registrars:

  • Registrar A: $0.99 first year, $14.99 renewal → 3-year TCO: $30.97
  • Registrar B: $9.99 first year, $11.99 renewal → 3-year TCO: $33.97
  • Registrar C: $9.99 first year, $9.99 renewal → 3-year TCO: $29.97

Registrar A looks cheapest until year two. Registrar C wins on total cost despite a higher upfront price. The difference is small here, but for premium TLDs the gap can be hundreds of dollars.

How to spot a promo trap

The rule of thumb is simple: if the renewal price is 5× or more the first-year price, you are looking at a promo trap. A $0.99 first year with a $5+ renewal is the classic pattern. Always check the renewal column and the 3-year total cost of ownership (TCO), not just the headline price.

Practical tips

  1. Always look at 3-year TCO, not first-year price. That is the number that matters for anything you plan to keep.
  2. Compare across registrars. The same TLD can vary by $5–$10/year between providers. Domain Hunter pulls live prices from Porkbun and Cloudflare plus weekly snapshots from Dynadot, Spaceship, ValueDomain, reg.ru, and Beget.
  3. Watch for coupon stacking. Some registrars allow combining a first-year promo with a renewal coupon. Domain Hunter applies known coupons automatically.
  4. Transfer out before renewal if your current registrar's renewal price is inflated. Most registrars allow transfers, and the receiving registrar usually charges standard registration price.
